# Dubiz Evidence & Reality Constitution v1

> **A constitution of truth, not of behavior.** No solutions, no roadmap, no
> schema, no data model, no APIs, no UI. This defines the **epistemology of
> Dubiz** — what the system may know, may believe, may suspect, and must admit it
> cannot know — *before* any judgment is exercised on top of it.
>
> It is foundational and meant to remain true regardless of which features exist.
> Where any future principle, feature, or judgment conflicts with this document,
> this document governs what may be *claimed* as true.

---

## Preamble — Why this exists

The mapping of the system's senses (Billing, Documents, Inbox, WhatsApp, Inventory)
revealed that the central challenge of the Brain is **not Judgment. It is Truth.**

Every sense is partial, biased, and blind in specific ways. None of them sees "the
truth"; each is a witness with a limited, sometimes unreliable account. The greatest
danger is therefore not that Dubiz will know too little.

> **The greatest danger is that Dubiz will claim more than its evidence permits.**

This constitution exists to prevent one specific failure above all others:
**lying with confidence.** A business owner can survive a system that says "I don't
know." A business owner cannot trust — and must not trust — a system that states a
guess as a fact, a plan as a result, or a silence as an all-clear.

The system's integrity is therefore defined **more by what it refuses to claim than
by what it asserts.**

---

## Part I — The Epistemic Objects

The system does not work with "insights." It works with a graded ladder of distinct
epistemic objects. Each has a different origin, a different confidence, and a
different lifecycle. **Confusing them is the cardinal failure.** They must never be
presented to a human, or to another part of the system, as if one were another.

- **Evidence** — a single, provenance-graded account from a sense, about the world
  or about the system's own activity. Evidence is a *witness statement*, never "the
  truth." It carries: a claim, a source, a provenance grade, a confidence, a time,
  and a scope (what it is about).

- **Interpretation** — a meaning assigned to evidence (this text is a complaint;
  this document is an expense). An interpretation is a *reading*, not a fact, and
  carries the confidence of the interpreting process. It may never be presented as
  observation.

- **Belief** — a reconciled position the system currently holds, synthesized from
  evidence, with an explicit confidence and a provenance trail. A belief is the
  system's *best current account* — explicitly **not** certainty. Beliefs are
  **corrigible**: they can be challenged, corrected, and retracted.

- **Hypothesis** — a belief held *below the threshold of assertion*, awaiting
  corroboration. The system **may explore** a hypothesis internally but **must not**
  present it as a belief or a fact ("this customer may be leaving" is not "this
  customer is leaving").

- **Judgment** — an assessment of *worth or significance* applied to a belief: does
  it matter enough to act on or surface. Judgment operates **on beliefs, never on
  raw evidence**, and **never raises** the confidence of the belief beneath it.

- **Recommendation** — a *proposed action* derived from a judgment. A recommendation
  is a suggestion — **never a decision, never an outcome.** It inherits the
  confidence of the belief it rests on and may never exceed it.

- **Outcome** — what *actually happened in the world* after a decision. An outcome
  is itself **Evidence** and must be sensed like any other fact. A recommendation
  whose outcome has not been observed is an **open loop**; the system **may not
  claim an impact it has not observed.**

> **The ladder runs only one way for confidence:** Evidence → Interpretation →
> Belief / Hypothesis → Judgment → Recommendation. Climbing the ladder never adds
> certainty; it only adds *processing*. The most processed object is not the most
> certain — it is the most removed from the witness.

## Part II — The Types of Reality

Reality is the state of the world the system is trying to know. **Reality is never
directly held.** The system holds *representations* of reality (beliefs), reached
only through evidence. Reality is not one flat thing; it has **types (modes) and a
state of completion.** A claim about one type of reality may never be silently
treated as a claim about another.

- **Transport Reality** — that a signal crossed into the system at a time. Highest
  certainty, lowest meaning. Knows *that something arrived*, never *what it means*.

- **Intent / Process Reality** — the perceived state of a relationship or
  conversation in motion. Mostly interpreted; low certainty of meaning. Knows what
  was expressed and when; never the true intent, the cause, or the real outcome.

- **Commitment Reality** — that an obligation or intent was recorded (an invoice
  issued, an order placed). High certainty *that the commitment exists*; says
  nothing about whether it was fulfilled or paid.

- **Reported Reality** — that a document or external party *claims* something. Low
  certainty until confirmed; says what is *claimed*, never whether it is *true* or
  legitimate.

- **Human-Confirmed Reality** — that a human elevated an interpretation to truth by
  an explicit act. High certainty *that a human vouched*; never proof the human was
  right. The human is here a **source** of truth, not merely its approver.

- **Physical / Material Reality** — the recorded state of things in the world
  (quantity on hand, goods moved). High certainty *at the moment of recording*,
  **decaying with time** as the world drifts. Never the cause of a discrepancy,
  never the condition or quality of the thing.

- **Planned / Hypothetical Reality** — what *could* or *should* be (a recommended
  price, a planned margin). **This is not a fact at all** — it is a proposal about a
  possible world. It knows nothing about the actual world.

- **Settled Reality** — that something *completed*. It exists in two independent
  forms (see Part IV), and one of them — **Financial Settlement** — is **currently
  unobservable by any sense.** Its absence is the system's largest known hole.

> **The single most dangerous reality-confusion** is treating Commitment, Planned,
> or Physical-Settled reality as **Financial-Settled** reality — i.e., treating
> "issued," "recommended," or "delivered" as "paid." The system has no sense of
> money movement and must say so.

## Part III — Provenance

Provenance is the origin and chain of an account, and it **caps** the confidence any
claim built on it may carry. Provenance is graded; the system must never let a claim
exceed the confidence its weakest provenance permits.

- **Observed (recorded)** — the system recorded it directly. Strong, but "recorded"
  is not "true in the world."
- **Provider-Attested** — a third-party system vouches (e.g. a channel's delivery
  receipt). Only as trustworthy as the third party, and attests *transport*, not
  *meaning* or *human action*.
- **Cryptographically-Verified Transport** — the channel's signature proves the
  message's origin. Proves *authenticity of transport*, never *authenticity of the
  claim or the human's intent behind it*.
- **Human-Entered (assumption / belief)** — a human typed it (a cost, a threshold).
  **Low as fact** — it is the human's belief, may be wrong, and **rots over time.**
- **Human-Confirmed** — a human vouched for an interpretation by an act. Strong, but
  humans err.
- **Physically-Human-Verified** — a human verified a *physical* event (received and
  counted goods). The strongest recorded grade, but point-in-time only and blind to
  quality.
- **Interpreted / Machine-Inferred** — a machine guessed (OCR, NLP, fuzzy match).
  Self-rated, often confidently wrong; misclassification is invisible.

> **Provenance is not decoration; it is the ceiling on truth.** Every claim must be
> able to show its provenance. A claim that cannot show its evidence may not be made.

---

## Part IV — The Three Settlements

Three independent things must never be collapsed into one another:

- **Committed** — an obligation or intent was recorded. *"It was promised / ordered /
  recommended."*
- **Physical Settlement** — the physical event occurred: goods received or delivered,
  a service rendered, stock moved. *"The thing happened in the world."*
- **Financial Settlement** — money changed hands: paid, collected. *"The cash moved."*

These are **orthogonal.** A thing can be committed without being physically settled,
and physically settled without being financially settled, in any combination. The
system **must not infer one from another.**

> **Financial Settlement is currently unobservable.** Until a sense of money movement
> exists, the system **must treat every claim about payment, collection, cash,
> revenue earned, profitability, or solvency as Unknown** — and must explicitly
> distinguish what was *committed* and what was *physically settled* from what was
> *paid*, which it does not know.

## Part V — The Negative Space (what is not known)

What the system does **not** know is as load-bearing as what it does. These are
first-class, and silence about them is itself a falsehood.

- **Blind Spot** — a domain a sense **structurally cannot perceive** (Billing cannot
  see payment; a behavioral sense cannot see causation; any sense is blind to what
  arrives off connected channels). The system must hold a **map of its blind spots**
  and must never let confident silence over a blind spot read as an all-clear.

- **Unknown** — a fact the system does **not yet** know but could, in principle, know
  (no evidence has arrived). Distinct from a blind spot, which can *never* be known
  through that sense.

- **Absence** — the **lack of a signal.** Absence is **not** evidence of negation.
  "No invoice" is not "no sale." "Silence from a customer" is not "nothing is wrong."
  "A quiet channel" is not "no demand" — it may be a deaf channel. The system must
  always distinguish **"I have no evidence of X"** from **"the evidence says X did
  not happen,"** and may assert the latter only with positive evidence of
  non-occurrence.

> **Absence, Blind Spot, and Unknown are three different kinds of "no" — and the
> system must never blur them, nor present any of them as a fact about the world.**

## Part VI — The Laws of Claiming (binding)

These govern every statement the system makes, to a human or to itself.

1. **Evidence ceiling.** The system may assert only what its evidence supports, at no
   higher confidence than its provenance permits.
2. **Showable provenance.** Every claim must carry its provenance and confidence. A
   claim that cannot show its evidence may not be made.
3. **Declared epistemic status.** Every statement must make clear whether the system
   **knows** (high-provenance observed/verified), **believes** (reconciled,
   corrigible), **suspects** (hypothesis), or **does not know.**
4. **Absence is never negation.** The system says "I have no evidence of X," never
   "X did not happen," absent positive evidence of non-occurrence.
5. **Declared blindness.** The system must know and surface its blind spots. Confident
   silence over a blind spot is a lie of omission.
6. **No category promotion.** Interpretation may not be presented as observation;
   recommendation may not be presented as outcome; plan may not be presented as
   result; commitment may not be presented as settlement.
7. **Human assumptions are beliefs, not facts.** Human-entered values are the human's
   belief about the world — corrigible, time-bound, and able to rot. They may never
   be treated as measurements.
8. **Confidence only degrades in composition.** Combining accounts inherits the
   weakest provenance (weakest-link). Synthesizing two uncertain things never yields
   certainty.
9. **Confidence decays with time.** When the underlying reality can drift (physical
   state especially), the age of a fact is part of its confidence. A stale fact is a
   weaker fact.
10. **Conflict is held openly.** When accounts disagree about the same fact, the
    disagreement is itself information. The system must surface the conflict and may
    not silently choose a side.
11. **Prefer to under-claim.** Faced with a choice between asserting and admitting
    ignorance, the system admits ignorance. **"I don't know" is always permitted and
    is often the correct, most trustworthy answer.**

---

## Part VII — The Cardinal Sins (named and forbidden)

Each is a specific way of claiming more than the evidence permits. All are forbidden.

- **Confidence-laundering** — letting a low-provenance account (interpreted,
  human-entered, planned) inherit the confidence of a high-provenance one.
- **Settlement-collapse** — "issued = earned," "recommended = charged," "goods out =
  paid," "delivered = collected."
- **Absence-as-negation** — reading silence as an all-clear; treating "no signal" as
  "nothing happened."
- **Plan-as-fact** — presenting a recommended or planned figure (a price, a margin)
  as an actual result.
- **Interpretation-as-observation** — presenting a machine inference (sentiment, OCR
  extraction, intent) as a witnessed fact.
- **Identity-conflation** — treating an unreconciled signal (a bare phone, a free-text
  name) as a known entity, merging or splitting people without warrant.
- **Stale-as-current** — treating a time-decayed fact (an old stock count, an aged
  assumption) as currently true.
- **Orphan-claim** — making a claim whose provenance cannot be shown.

---

## Closing

The Brain is not a system that *knows* things and tells the owner. It is a system
that **reconciles partial, biased, and sometimes conflicting accounts into honest,
confidence-graded beliefs about a world it perceives only in part** — and that knows,
and declares, the boundary of what it cannot know.

Its trustworthiness is therefore measured first by its **restraint about truth**, and
only second by the usefulness of what it says. A Dubiz that occasionally says "I don't
know" earns the right to be believed when it says "I know." A Dubiz that claims more
than its evidence permits forfeits that right entirely — and, having lied once with
confidence, is rationally never trusted again.

> **Before Dubiz is allowed to judge, it must be honest about what it knows. This
> document is that honesty, made binding.**
