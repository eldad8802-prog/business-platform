# Dubiz Brain — Belief Formation Constitution v1

> **A conceptual constitution only.** No code, no schema, no migrations, no UI, no
> API, no signals, no Hero, no product behavior. It fixes the conceptual law of
> **how Beliefs come into being** in the Dubiz Brain — derived from the Belief
> Formation Review and the Expectation & Norm Review.
>
> It governs only the transition into, and the life of, Beliefs inside the World
> Model. It sits beneath the Evidence & Reality Constitution and the Brain Internal
> Architecture, and above any future implementation, which it does **not** prescribe.

---

## Part I — The Nature of Belief Formation

**1. Evidence → Belief is not automatic.** Evidence is always remembered; a Belief
is formed only when there is **warrant**. The default is **to remember without
believing.**

**2. Perceive ≠ Understand.** Evidence is *perception* (what the system received).
Belief is *warranted understanding* (a stance the system is entitled to hold). The
two are different acts.

**3. Belief formation is the exception, not the rule.** Most Evidence never becomes
a Belief, and this is correct — it is the Evidence & Reality Constitution's
"prefer to under-claim," in motion.

---

## Part II — The Legal Paths to a Belief

**4. There are four legal paths to forming a Belief:**
- **Adoption** — accept a single source's claim (one account, no contradiction,
  sufficient provenance).
- **Reconciliation** — resolve multiple/conflicting claims about one proposition; the
  resulting confidence reflects the conflict.
- **Inference** — derive a belief no source asserted, from *present* claims/beliefs
  (synthesis, correlation, deduction).
- **Expectation-Violation** — derive a belief from the *absence* of an expected
  outcome, measured against a warranted Norm (Part VI).

**5. Human Assertion is Adoption with human provenance**, not a separate path. A
human-entered value is the human's claim, adopted with `Human-Entered` /
`Human-Confirmed` provenance.

**6. Priors / Defaults are forbidden unless grounded.** Believing something in the
absence of grounds is forbidden. A prior is legitimate **only** if it is a learned,
evidence-based second-order belief — in which case it is Inference from history, not
a free assumption.

---

## Part III — When a Belief Must Not Form

**7. A Belief must not be formed when any of these holds** (the correct result is
Unknown):
- provenance too weak to hold a stance,
- irreducible conflict (forming would mean silently choosing a side),
- no grounds at all,
- scope / identity unresolved (no clear referent),
- a known blind spot (the proposition is in a domain the grounds cannot perceive),
- absence without a warranted expectation,
- grounds stale or decayed below the holding threshold.

> The unifying rule: **a belief may not form when forming it would require claiming
> more than the grounds permit** — i.e., when it would breach a Cardinal Safety Rule.

**8. Unknown is a legitimate and positive state.** Unknown is not a failure; it is
the **correct default** whenever warrant is absent. The Brain has both the right and
the duty to remain at Unknown.

---

## Part IV — Unknown, Hypothesis, Belief

**9. Unknown is not a weak Belief.**
- **Unknown** = *no belief object exists* for the proposition.
- **Hypothesis** = a *weak, grounded* held belief, below the assertion threshold.
- **Belief** = an *assertable* held proposition.

Conflating Unknown with a low-confidence Hypothesis is Absence-as-Negation in
reverse: "I have no basis" is not "I weakly suspect."

**10. There are two thresholds on the confidence continuum:**
- **Formation threshold:** Unknown → Hypothesis (enough grounds to hold a tentative
  stance, not enough to assert).
- **Assertion threshold:** Hypothesis → Belief (enough to assert and reason with).

These thresholds create three qualitatively distinct regimes with different
permissions: Unknown (may say "I don't know," may seek; may not assert/act),
Hypothesis (may explore; may not present as belief or act as if true), Belief (may
assert, reason, and feed to Judgment).

---

## Part V — The Life of a Belief

**11. A Belief, once formed, is not static. Its lifecycle includes:** persist,
strengthen, weaken, decay, retract / die, revise / replace, merge / split, promote /
demote across bands.

**12. Beliefs about drift-prone reality decay if not refreshed.** A belief about a
fluid state (e.g., a stock level) loses confidence over time toward Unknown. A belief
about an immutable event (e.g., that an invoice was issued on a date) does not decay
the same way. A belief's persistence is **continuously re-earned, never inertial.**

**13. Birthright Conditions — every Belief must be born with all of:**
- **Traceability** — to its grounds (Evidence, or beliefs that bottom out in Evidence),
- **Corrigibility** — it can be challenged, revised, retracted,
- **Temporal grounding** — anchored in time,
- **Scope clarity** — a resolved referent,
- **Provenance-bounded confidence** — held no higher than its provenance permits,
- **Blind-spot respect** — not about a domain its grounds cannot perceive,
- **Conflict honesty** — if formed amid conflict, the conflict is encoded in its
  confidence, never hidden.

A belief lacking any of these may not be born.

**14. The deep threshold.** Forming a Belief is the moment the system becomes
**willing to be wrong about the world** — on the basis of sufficient grounds and
made safe by corrigibility. Evidence cannot be wrong (it only records an account); a
Belief can be wrong (it commits to a stance). The threshold is the justified
acceptance of fallibility.

---

## Part VI — Norms and Expectations

**15. A Norm is not one thing.** It must be split into **Descriptive Norm** and
**Prescriptive Norm.**

**16. Descriptive Norm:**
- a belief about a *regularity* ("orders usually produce an invoice within 5 days"),
- lives **inside** the World Model (scope = class),
- learned from Evidence / history / outcomes,
- falsifiable,
- confidence-based.

**17. Prescriptive Norm:**
- a *standard / binding rule* ("an order must produce an invoice"),
- lives **outside** the World Model,
- grounded in **authority** — law, the business owner, policy, process,
- **not created by the Brain**,
- **not falsifiable** by reality (a violation is a breach, not a refutation).

**18. The Brain may create Descriptive Norms. The Brain may not create Prescriptive
Norms** — it has no authority to decide what *ought* to be.

**19. Prescription-from-Description is forbidden.** "X usually happens" may never be
turned into "X must happen." A deviation from a descriptive norm is an **anomaly
worth noticing**, not a **violation worth asserting**.

**20. Norm vs Expectation:**
- **Norm** = a general rule (about a class).
- **Expectation** = a Norm applied to a specific case, with a specific scope and a
  time window ("order #123 should have an invoice by date X").

An Expectation can fail in two ways even when checked correctly: the **Norm** may be
unwarranted, or the **instantiation** may be wrong (mismatched case, mis-derived
deadline).

---

## Part VII — Expectation-Violation (the most dangerous path)

**21. Absence becomes a legitimate Expectation-Violation only if ALL of these hold:**
- a **warranted Norm** exists,
- a **positive, observed trigger** exists (the condition is a fact, not itself an
  absence),
- the **instantiation is correct** (this case truly matches the norm; scope resolved),
- a **deadline / time window has demonstrably elapsed**,
- **perceptual coverage**: *if the outcome had occurred, the system would have seen
  it.*

**22. Perceptual Coverage is the critical guard.** Without it, absence remains
**Unknown**. Absence is evidence of non-occurrence only in a domain the system
reliably perceives. ("No invoice" can be informative; "no payment" cannot — payment
is a blind spot.)

**23. An Expectation-Violation forms a Belief only about the *fact of the gap.***
- Legitimate belief: "an invoice was due and was not found."
- **Not** licensed: any belief about the *cause* — "there is a process problem," "the
  customer churned" — these are **Hypotheses** requiring further Inference, and the
  cause is usually a blind spot (causal blindness). For a prescriptive-norm violation
  the gap-fact may be high-confidence; for a descriptive-norm violation it is weaker
  (it is inference-on-inference) and is at most an anomaly.

**24. Expectation-Violation is kept as a separate path** even though it is a kind of
Inference — for safety. It is the only path that reasons from *what was not seen*,
so it requires unique guards that must never be lost by folding it into ordinary
Inference.

**25. Cardinal Sins specific to Expectation-Violation:**
- Absence-as-Negation,
- Invented Expectations,
- Hidden Norms,
- Learned Bias,
- Silent Promotion (treating a gap as a confident belief about cause),
- False Deadlines,
- Intent-from-Delay,
- Prescription-from-Description,
- Coverage Blindness.

---

## Part VIII — Scope of this Constitution

**26. Belief Formation feeds the World Model only.** It does not perform Judgment,
does not produce Recommendations, and does not decide Delivery. It governs **only**
when a Belief may be **created, updated, strengthened, weakened, or removed.**

---

## 27. Non-Implementation Status

This document **does not implement the Brain.** It does not define a schema, a data
model, signals, APIs, UI, or product behavior. It does not specify confidence
formulas, thresholds, or algorithms. It fixes **only the conceptual constitution of
how Beliefs form** in the Dubiz Brain — the warrant, the paths, the prohibitions, the
lifecycle, and the law of Norms and Expectations — as the stable foundation that
future design and implementation must honor, but which it deliberately leaves
unspecified.
