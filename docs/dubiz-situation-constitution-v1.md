# Dubiz Brain — Situation Constitution v1

> **A conceptual constitution only.** No code, no schema, no signals, no UI, no
> Hero, no product behavior. It fixes the conceptual nature of the **Situation** —
> the coherent unit of meaning that Judgment consumes — by integrating the Situation
> Review and the Situation Coherence Review.
>
> It sits beneath the Evidence & Reality Constitution, the Belief Formation
> Constitution, the Brain Internal Architecture, and the Judgment Constitution, and
> above any future implementation, which it does **not** prescribe.

---

## Part I — What a Situation Is

**1. A Situation is a Composite Subject** — an emergent referent of the form "a
state-of-affairs," constituted by a coherent cluster of Beliefs. It answers **"what
is going on?"** as Party answers **"who is this?"**

**2. A Situation is not a Belief, not a Condition, not an Awareness Object.** A Belief
is a single held proposition; a Condition is a single vital-sign reading; an Awareness
Object is the egress packaging of something judged worth attention. A Situation is the
coherent cluster itself.

**3. A Situation is not a Primitive.** It has no atom of its own. Its constituents are
Beliefs (the atom of the World Model). A Situation is a *structure over* Belief atoms.

**4. A Situation is the parallel of Party.** Both are emergent subjects constituted by
belief clusters, both have identity over time, both have a lifecycle (merge / split /
resolve). Party clusters beliefs by **identity**; a Situation clusters beliefs by
**coherence**.

| | Party | Situation |
|---|---|---|
| question | "who is this?" | "what is going on?" |
| constituted from | identity-beliefs | substantive-beliefs |
| clustering criterion | identity | coherence |
| atom of its own | none | none |
| lifecycle | merge / split / retract | arise / grow / shrink / evolve / resolve / split / merge |

**5. A Situation lives inside the World Model** as an emergent subject — not as a
projection (that is Business Status), not created outside it. Beliefs may be *about* a
Situation (a Situation is a legal Scope, per the Belief Formation model).

**6. A Situation is the input unit of Judgment.** Judgment assesses Worth on coherent
units of meaning; an isolated Belief is the degenerate case (a Situation of one). The
egress is therefore, precisely:
```
World Model (beliefs) → [coherence] Situations → Judgment → Awareness Objects → Recommendations
```

**7. A Situation may rest on Unknowns.** It is constituted by whatever beliefs cohere,
including second-order (blind-spot) beliefs and declared gaps. A "blind-spot
situation" is legitimate; positive beliefs are not required.

## Part II — Coherence: how Beliefs constitute one Situation

**8. The critical principle — Coherence is not a hard relation. Coherence is a
Belief.** There is no clean deterministic signal (unlike Party's tax-id or phone).
The binding that makes several beliefs "one situation" is itself a **corrigible
coherence-belief**, with grounds, confidence, corrigibility, and a lifecycle.

**9. The system never holds "these are the same situation." It holds "there is
currently warrant to believe these are part of the same situation."** This single
distinction is what prevents Narrative Fabrication and keeps Situations fully
consistent with the Evidence & Reality and Belief Formation constitutions.

**10. The legitimate forms of a coherence-belief are causal / explanatory
entanglement:**
- **common-cause** (several beliefs as symptoms of one underlying state),
- **joint-effect** (several beliefs jointly converging on one outcome).

(Mere extensional identity — the same fact framed several ways — is not coherence; it
is reconciliation into one Belief.)

**11. Coherence is bounded by two constraints:**
- a **Party axis** (same-subject coherence is one common — but neither necessary nor
  sufficient — form),
- a **Time constraint** (constituents must share a temporal envelope; co-occurring or
  causally sequenced).

Neither alone defines a Situation.

**12. Because coherence is causal / inferential — and the Brain is structurally weak
at causation — a coherence-belief is usually tentative (a Hypothesis), held at low
confidence.** It must be surfaced honestly as "these may be related," never as
established fact.

**13. Default Separate.** Beliefs are separate unless a warranted coherence-belief
binds them. Binding requires meeting the Belief Formation bar; loose association
("both relate to profitability") does not qualify. This is what keeps a Situation from
expanding to contain everything.

## Part III — The Situation Resolution Engine

**14. There is a Situation Resolution Engine, parallel in structure to the Party
Resolution Engine** — it produces corrigible binding-beliefs, supports merge and
split, and gates by warrant.

**15. It is epistemically the opposite of Party Resolution.** Party Resolution binds
on strong deterministic signals (high confidence); Situation Resolution binds on
causal / explanatory entanglement (low confidence, usually hypothesis). Therefore the
Situation Resolution Engine must be **far more conservative** — its default is to
**not** bind, and it holds its bindings as **tentative**.

---

## Part IV — Lifecycle

**16. A Situation's lifecycle is richer than a Belief's, because two things change:
the coherence-binding and the constituent set.**
- **Arises** — a coherence-belief forms above the formation threshold.
- **Grows / Shrinks** — beliefs attach (a new symptom) or detach (a constituent retracts).
- **Strengthens / Weakens** — the binding's confidence changes with evidence.
- **Evolves** — the situation's state updates as constituents move.
- **Resolves** — the state-of-affairs no longer holds; the binding's grounds disappear;
  graceful death.
- **Splits / Merges** — see below.

**17. A Situation splits** when the coherence-belief is revised to "these are actually
two states-of-affairs" — typically when new evidence reveals the binding was an
over-merge.

**18. Situations merge** when a coherence-belief forms that two tracked situations are
actually one — typically when new evidence reveals a shared cause.

**19. Nesting is permitted only shallowly.** A Situation may contain sub-Situations,
but each level of nesting is another coherence-belief (causal-over-causal), so
confidence degrades by weakest-link. Shallow nesting may be warranted; deep nesting is
almost never warranted and is an epistemic smell.

---

## Part V — Cardinal Risk and Sins

**20. Narrative Fabrication is the cardinal risk of Situations.** The Situation
Resolution Engine is precisely where the Brain is most tempted to bind beliefs into a
causally appealing but unwarranted story. The "narrative shaping" prohibition is
located here.

**21. The cardinal sins of Situation formation:**
- **Over-Merge** — binding beliefs into one situation without warrant (false coherence /
  fabricated narrative).
- **False-Causation** — binding by an assumed cause the system is structurally blind to.
- **Over-Confidence** — presenting a tentative coherence-belief as an established fact.

**22. The discipline against all three** is Part II: every binding is a
warranted, confidence-bounded, corrigible coherence-belief; the default is separate;
and the system says "there is currently warrant to believe these cohere," never "these
are the same situation."

---

## Part VI — Scope and Non-Implementation

**23. Scope.** This Constitution governs only what constitutes a Situation and how it
is formed, evolved, and dissolved. It does not perform Judgment (it produces
Judgment's input), does not produce Awareness Objects (those are judged-and-packaged
Situations), and does not produce Recommendations.

**24. Non-Implementation Status.** This document does not implement the Brain, define a
schema, signals, ingestion or coherence mechanics, UI, Hero, or product behavior. It
specifies no algorithm, threshold, or confidence formula. It fixes **only the
conceptual constitution of the Situation** — its nature as a composite subject, the
coherence-belief that constitutes it, the conservative Resolution Engine, its
lifecycle, and the sins it must avoid — as the stable foundation that future design and
implementation must honor, but which it deliberately leaves unspecified.
